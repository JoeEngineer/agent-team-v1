# Reviewer severity calibration: Claude vs Codex (A7)

Item A7 of [the consolidated plan](2026-11-03-consolidated-plan.md). Question: is the Codex run's low first-review approval rate (6/20, vs 5/6 for Claude's access-errors) a real quality gap, or do the two reviewers use different severity thresholds?

Sources: `minio-s3-sync-codex/.agent-team/logs/reviews.jsonl` (45 distinct reviews, guidance 0.4.0–0.4.4), the Claude access-errors archive `reviews.jsonl` (32 distinct reviews over hardening and access-errors), the generated Codex agent file `.codex/agents/reviewer-*.toml`, and `agents/reviewer.md`.

## Answer

**Calibration explains a small part of the gap. Most of it is real.**
- **Codex.** Read against the written definition, 25 of the 34 majors are clearly blocking. 9 are borderline, and 7 of those are about docs. Calling the borderline ones minor would turn 2 Codex send-backs into approvals (IS-7 a1, HD-6 a1), so 6/20 becomes 8/20.
- **Claude.** Its reviewers under-call: about 5 of its 52 minors meet the definition of major.
- **The two tools differ most on minors.** Codex reviewers almost never report them: 4 in 45 reviews. Claude's report 52 in 32. That breaks fold-ins on Codex, and it makes minor counts useless for comparing the two tools.

## 1. Same instructions on both sides

The Codex reviewer's `developer_instructions` are `agents/reviewer.md` word for word, severity rules included (generated by 0.4.4). Both reviewers got the same rubric. The Codex review briefs are encrypted in the transcripts (only the TASK and WHY lines are readable), so I can't rule out that a brief asked for blocking findings only. Nothing in the readable lines suggests it.

## 2. Severity mix

| | Reviews | Major | Minor | Minors per review | Approvals with zero findings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Codex (Terra/high, finals Sol/high) | 45 | 34 | 4 | 0.09 | 25 of 27 |
| Claude (Sonnet/high, finals Opus/high) | 32 | 3 | 52 | 1.6 | 12 of 29, nearly all re-reviews after a fold-in |

Every Codex send-back lists only majors, and Codex approvals almost never list anything. The rubric says "List every finding you would raise with a human author, minors included". The Codex reviewers don't follow it.

## 3. Codex majors against the definition

The definition says a major is blocking because it changes behaviour, breaks an acceptance criterion, or makes a test not prove what it claims.

**Clearly major (25):**

| Kind | Count | Tasks |
|---|---:|---|
| Security | 3 | HD-1 ×2, HD-4 |
| Correctness, behaviour or data | 13 | HD-2 ×2, HD-3, HD-4, AE-1 ×4, the plugin's codex-log.js ×2, IS-1 (criterion), HD-5 (criterion), IS-7 a2 (documented restore deletes state before validating) |
| Test doesn't prove its claim | 8 | IS-4, IS-6, HD-3, HD-5 ×2, HD-7, HD-8 ×2 |
| Test can hang CI | 1 | AE-2 (subprocess with no timeout) |

**Borderline (9):**
- **Docs inaccuracy or gap (7):**
  - IS-7 a1: a regex example with an extra backslash, a missing restore procedure, and an incomplete shutdown guarantee.
  - IS-7 a2: restore verification can report success without proving a scan completed.
  - HD-6 ×2: redaction not documented, and an over-broad shutdown bound.
  - Plugin `codex.md`: a recovery claim that is too broad.
- **Coverage gap, not a test that fails to prove its claim (2):** IS-4 (recovery never verified) and IS-6 (immediate-consumption boundary not tested).

The rubric has nothing for docs except documented commands, and nothing for "a test that should exist but doesn't". Those two gaps are where the Codex reviewer goes major.

## 4. Claude minors that meet the definition of major

- **HD-7:** the source outage test doesn't assert that its precondition was reached. By the rubric, that is a test that doesn't prove what it claims.
- **HD-5:** read-side attribution fires on an unrelated outage. That is a behaviour bug, called minor as a "prefer…".
- **AE-2:** the unit suite misses the under-15 s acceptance criterion. It was called minor because the criterion was already unmet before the PR. The rubric has no "pre-existing" exception.
- **AE-final:** a 0-byte object can count as KMS evidence. It was called "Unverified" and minor, and the Director had to fix it in AE-6.
- **AE-final, README:69:** says the hold limit never affects whether a file is charged. That is false, and users tune the setting from it.

Claude reviewers lean toward "approve with minors", which `merging.md` treats as "may merge as is". That turns some real defects into optional fold-ins.

## 6. Recommendation

Applied in plugin 0.4.7. The Coach can compare reviews before and after by `guidance_version`.

**Change the rubric in `agents/reviewer.md`, which both tools use.** Add these lines under "Severity":

```diff
 - `major` — blocking: changes behaviour, breaks an acceptance criterion, or makes a test not prove what it claims.
 - `critical` — blocking and dangerous: data loss, security, or breaks the build for everyone.
+
+Calibration (apply these the same way every time):
+- A test that doesn't assert its precondition was reached, or doesn't observe the state after the event, is `major`, in unit and integration tests alike.
+- A missing test for a path the acceptance criteria or EDGE CASES name is `major`. A missing test for a path they don't name is `minor`.
+- An acceptance criterion the PR leaves unmet is `major`. If it was already unmet before the PR, show that (the base's result) and make it `minor` with a follow-up.
+- Docs: a wrong statement a user would act on (a guarantee, a config value or example that won't work, a restated decision rule) is `major`. Incomplete or stale wording, labels and cosmetic differences in examples are `minor`.
+- A behaviour bug is `major` even when it is rare, unless a decision record accepts it (cite it). "Unverified" concerns you couldn't confirm are `minor`, but say what would confirm them.
+- Test or CI plumbing: a missing timeout or cleanup that can hang CI or leak shared resources is `major`. One that only matters after a test has already failed is `minor`.
```

**Make minors mandatory on approvals.** Add to step 6:

```diff
+   An approval with no findings should be rare. Before approving with none, look once more for docs, style and coverage nits, and say in `summary` that you found none.
```

**Codex briefs (`skills/director/references/codex.md`, review task DELIVER line):** add "List minor findings too." The rubric already says so, but Codex reviewers skip it. A line in the brief is cheap and can be checked from the logs.

**Coach (`agents/coach.md`):** first-review approval rates are not comparable across tools without a calibration check. When comparing tools, count blocking findings by the rubric's kinds (security, behaviour, test-not-proving, criterion) instead of comparing raw verdicts.

## Caveats

- One reader (me) classified the findings. The borderline calls are judgment.
- The two runs split the milestones into different tasks and used different reviewer models (Terra/high vs Sonnet/high). They aren't a controlled comparison.
- The Claude archive covers hardening and access-errors only. The Codex log covers all three milestones.
- The Codex briefs are encrypted, so what they asked reviewers to report is unverified.
